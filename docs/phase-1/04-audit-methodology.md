# Brand Audit Methodology
Status: Proposed for review | Version 0.1

## 1. Purpose and boundary
Provide a reproducible, scope-aware audit of observable brand touchpoints, consistency, credibility, accessibility and documented strategy. Identify what exists, what conflicts, what is unsupported, and what should be decided or repaired. An audit is not proof of brand-market effectiveness, nor a substitute for interviews or quantitative customer research.

## 2. Scope contract
Before inspection, record company websites/domains, products, markets, languages, official accounts, likely channels, date, permitted source access, known private materials, and exclusions. Identify claimed universe of touchpoints and discovered universe. Audit public sources only when actually accessible. Record inaccessible and unknown surfaces rather than silently excluding them. Never claim exhaustive online coverage.

## 3. Discovery procedure
1. Start with verified official domain and supplied documents.
2. Discover linked channels (web pages, social profiles, directories, listings, app stores, newsletters, press/media, public customer proof) relevant to this company.
3. Distinguish official/owned, claimed, third-party, historical, and potentially impersonating accounts.
4. Sample key page types, languages, regions, product lines and high-impact touchpoints; log sampling frame and rationale.
5. Preserve evidence locator, URL, capture date, page title, excerpt/screenshot where permitted, and limitation.
6. Identify claims, terms, logos, voice, visual patterns, target segments, calls to action and proof across surfaces.
7. Compare against approved brand rules *if they exist*; otherwise flag as unresolved identity choices rather than arbitrarily choosing a standard.
8. Produce findings with remediation ownership.

## 4. Rubric (proposed, customizable before baseline)
| Dimension | Weight | Observable criteria |
|---|---:|---|
| Naming and positioning consistency | 20 | Entity name, category, tagline, claims, ICP, offer alignment |
| Verbal identity consistency | 15 | Tone, terminology, copy consistency, naming conventions |
| Visual system consistency | 15 | Logo variants, type, colors, composition, legibility |
| Cross-touchpoint coherence | 15 | Same approved identity across relevant owned channels and languages |
| Claims and credibility | 15 | Claims supported, proof dated, testimonials permissioned, no fictitious results |
| Application and asset readiness | 10 | Approved templates, usable logos, status/owner defined |
| Governance and maintainability | 10 | Approval, currency, owners, versioning, accessible authoritative rules |
**Total = 100.** These are a starting evaluation framework, not industry-validated psychometric weights. A brand with no approved strategy cannot be assigned a false low or high compliance score: use `not_assessable` until an applicable standard exists; record an identity-gap finding.

## 5. Scoring mechanics
Each applicable criterion receives a fixed rubric-defined score `0, 0.5, 1` based on reproducible evidence anchors: 0 = demonstrated noncompliance; 0.5 = partial/mixed compliance; 1 = demonstrated compliance. `not_applicable` excluded with rationale; `not_assessable` reported separately, not scored as zero or full marks. Weighted dimension result = sum(applicable criterion weight × criterion score)/sum(applicable criterion weight). Overall compliance = 100 × weighted achieved / weighted applicable. Publish dimension scores, denominator, rubric version, scope, and evidence.

Do **not** issue a numeric total when critical dimensions are not assessable or coverage is insufficient under predeclared minimums. A 100/100 score is attainable only for fully observed *applicable* compliance rules. It is not a score for differentiation, commercial success, customer love or market fit.

## 6. Coverage metrics (separate from score)
Record discovered touchpoints, audited touchpoints, omitted/inaccessible touchpoints, sample method, and coverage percentage only when denominator is defined. Track critical touchpoint coverage separately (home page, primary product/offer, major owned profiles, core sales collateral if available). Reaudit must disclose changed scope, inaccessible sources, and criterion comparability.

## 7. Finding severity and ownership
- **Critical:** deceptive/conflicting identity on a primary channel, materially unsupported claim, rights/privacy concern or severe user-facing issue; requires explicit owner escalation.
- **High:** prominent positioning conflict or major consistency failure.
- **Medium:** repeated inconsistency affecting clarity or execution.
- **Low:** localized formatting or minor application defect.
For each finding: observation, why it matters, precise evidence, affected surfaces, criterion, severity, disposition (`fix|investigate|accept_exception|not_applicable`), owner module, remediation action, verification procedure, status.

## 8. Remediation routing
- BA records observation and scores.
- BII resolves category/identity, audience, differentiation, founder/organizational-choice issues.
- BEG specifies approved content/design/usage rules and required assets.
- The **implementation owner** updates actual touchpoints; may be a human or separately authorized automation.
- BA independently rechecks published changes against the baseline, new approved rules, and maintained scope.
Distinguish `documented`, `approved`, `implemented`, and `verified` states.

## 9. Adversarial audit checks
Search for inconsistent category language, dual brand names, obsolete product names, broken logo variants, stale claims, contradictory customer promises, differing CTAs, missing proof, inaccessible core messages, unclear audience, and contradictory legal/entity descriptions. Test against sample pages and artifacts; do not assume all channels are relevant. Separate auditable facts from subjective strategic recommendations.

## 10. Reaudit and 100/100 handling
Require baseline reference, rubric version, audited universe comparison, fresh capture dates, each finding ID outcome and verification evidence. Score increases reflect verified changes only, not newly written guidance. If baseline and reaudit scopes differ, show comparable-subset score plus new-scope score; do not present a single unqualified improvement. A perfect compliance score with low coverage is not a successful audit.

## 11. Quality controls
Two independent passes on high-severity findings; evidence citations resolve; sampling decisions documented; rubric is locked between baseline and comparable reaudit; scores recomputable by deterministic script; uncertainty explicit. Report external relevance and brand effectiveness separately as hypotheses requiring market evidence.
