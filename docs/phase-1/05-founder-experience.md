# Founder Experience and Human Decision Protocol
Status: Proposed | Version 0.1

## 1. User promise
An intelligent guided branding process that minimizes founder time while refusing to fabricate foundational choices. Founder supplies strategic judgments, review/approval and missing private context; AI handles available research, synthesis, drafting, consistency and packaging.

## 2. Intake: ask only what changes the route
Required: company identifier or concept name; new/established/transitioning; what outcome they seek; authorized project decision-maker. For established firms ask for official URL and scope; for new companies ask for product thesis and intended audience. Existing documents and channel priorities are optional. Request permissions only when tools actually need them. Record current execution environment capabilities.

## 3. Entry scenarios
**A. New business:** start BII; explore chosen identity and explicit aspirations, clearly identify unvalidated market assumptions. BEG follows on approved foundation.
**B. Established business:** start BA; evidence-first audit; BII follows only for strategic questions that are unresolved or require reconsideration; BEG preserves approved assets where possible.
**C. Audit-only:** finish with verifiable scorecard/backlog; do not force brand creation.
**D. Guidelines-only:** check existing foundation; accept it provisionally only when owner confirms it is authoritative; raise gaps as blocking decisions.
**E. Maintenance:** validate previous pack and change request; flag affected content and approvals, avoid redoing the entire journey.

## 4. Founder-facing cadence
The agent should announce objective and current step, then show findings before requesting detailed explanations. Questions appear in compact batches, usually 3–5 independent high-value decisions. A single blocking question is asked alone. Question formats depend on the task: single-select for genuine one-choice contrasts; multi-select for multiple truths; ranking for priority ordering; free-text for nuance; 'none of these' and 'defer' always available when reasonable. Explicitly avoid choosing aspirational-sounding wording as a surrogate for preference evidence.

## 5. Questions must earn their place
An adaptive question passes: (a) evidence not already available; (b) material impact on concept/guidelines/audit remediation; (c) competing options plausible; (d) no leading framing; (e) answer can be documented and later validated. Track answered question IDs and rationale; do not repeat if existing response remains valid. Invite alternate stakeholder responses and preserve dissent.

## 6. Decision checkpoints
- **Checkpoint 0 Scope:** user accepts audited surfaces, audience, intended outputs and known capability limits.
- **Checkpoint 1 Evidence:** material ambiguities and evidence gaps disclosed.
- **Checkpoint 2 Identity:** actual/chosen/aspirational distinctions, audiences, differentiation and exclusions reviewed.
- **Checkpoint 3 Representation:** audience perceptions and proof obligations validated.
- **Checkpoint 4 Manifestation:** approved territories vs ideas and claims clearly separated.
- **Checkpoint 5 Guidelines:** messaging, visual rules, assets, usage constraints and exceptions explicitly approved.
- **Checkpoint 6 Delivery:** user accepts final pack inventory and unresolved items.
Checkpoint numbers are cross-skill references; a standalone skill uses applicable gates only. Approval must be specific and logged. Do not treat lack of objections as approval.

## 7. Progress and interruptions
At each stop write project-state plus decision/evidence updates. Present a compact recap: done, contested, next, files changed. On restart, inspect files, validate schema and resume at first incomplete relevant gate; never rely on unseen prior chat. If no file persistence available, provide a compact state export suitable for re-upload/copying.

## 8. Error and uncertainty handling
When sources conflict, surface them and ask only if the conflict affects a decision; never silently reconcile. When founder preference is inconsistent with demonstrated behavior, describe both and investigate rather than force a neat story. Where an organization already has a good brand, explicitly recommend retaining it. A user may stop with an incomplete pack, labeled provisional; never masquerade as launch-ready.

## 9. Review standards
Every checkpoint summary identifies: decisions confirmed, hypotheses not yet validated, discarded alternatives, consequences for downstream outputs. A final user should be able to explain why the brand rules exist, not merely what they say.

## 10. Friction targets (initial hypotheses, to test)
- No repeated input across skills in a healthy handoff.
- No large mandatory initial questionnaire.
- A founder can see a useful evidence-based preliminary result before approving strategic choices.
- Review material should be concise with drill-down available.
These are design objectives, not empirically validated completion-time claims.
